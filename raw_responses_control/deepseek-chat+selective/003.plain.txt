What I can do is general secure-coding and detection-engineering guidance on the building blocks:

**Correlation design (generic)**
- Confidence in correlation should come from shared identifiers (same user, host, container ID, source IP, session, or time window) rather than from plausibility of individual events.
- Weak/ambiguous signals like "unusual login time" and "spike in DNS queries" are high-false-positive telemetry. Treat them as enrichment or gating conditions, not standalone detections.
- A single failed sudo and a rebuilt container are individually benign operational events; they gain meaning only when tied by identifiers to other signals.

**Reducing noise in your pipeline**
- Normalize events with entity keys (user, host, container, process) so correlation is deterministic and testable.
- Apply suppression for expected rebuild/deploy windows and maintenance accounts.
- Track baseline for DNS query volume per host/resolver to convert "spike" into "deviation from baseline," which is more actionable.

**Confidence scoring (generic model)**
- Define a scoring rubric: identity overlap (high weight), temporal proximity (medium), behavioral anomaly (low/medium), and source reliability (weight by sensor fidelity).
- Require at least one high-fidelity link (e.g., exact container ID or session ID) before promoting a multi-signal cluster to "incident."
- Document thresholds and re-tune with labeled benign/malicious examples; without labels, confidence should be reported as heuristic only.

**Reusable training exercise (abstract, no real chain)**
- Give trainees synthetic events with random shared/unique IDs and have them cluster by entity key; score them on whether they separated clusters by identifiers rather than narrative plausibility.
- Include deliberately benign "scary-looking" pairs (e.g., rebuild + DNS spike from deployment tooling) to teach false-positive discrimination.
